## How to rehearse (the method that works)

1. **Aloud and standing.** Reading in your head is not rehearsal; your mouth needs the practice.
2. **Four stages, in order:** read the notes; use only bullets; use only slide titles and takeaways; use nothing. Move down a stage only when the one above is easy.
3. **Say the takeaway bar in your own words** before you leave each slide. That is the habit that keeps you on message.
4. **Never read the slide.** The slide is for the audience; you are the explanation.
5. **Pause for two seconds after a headline number.** Say it, stop, let it land: *0.379 to 0.881.*
6. **Time at checkpoints**, not just at the end. The clock should read about: slide 5 at 4 min, slide 16 at 17 min, slide 28 at 29 min, slide 33 at 34 min (the guide lists the exact running totals).
7. **Record yourself once a week** and count the filler words. Two minutes of listening is worth an hour of repeating.

## Answering questions (the formula)

1. **Listen to the end. Pause. Restate** the question in one short phrase if it is long.
2. **Answer first** in one sentence.
3. **Give one number** from the report.
4. **State the limit** honestly before they do.
5. **Stop.** Do not fill the silence.

**When you do not know:** *"I have not tested that. My expectation is X, because Y. It is on my list of future work, and I would test it by Z."* Never bluff; a clear "not tested" scores better than a vague answer.

**When the question is hostile:** keep your voice slow and say "that is a fair challenge" if it is. Then use the formula. Most hostile questions are one of: *isn't it trivial, is it significant, is it representative, did you tune fairly.* All four are in `QA_DRILL.md`.

**Use the backup slides.** Say "I have that in a backup slide", go to it (B1 to B10 are listed on the last slide), and answer from the table.
